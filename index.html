HORIZON · A FRESH FOUNDATION

A complete world of water.

This version starts with hydrology. It generates one closed planet, then resolves its ocean, drainage divides, headwaters, streams, rivers, ponds, lakes and spill outlets as a single connected system.

World
A fictional planet, 128 km in diameter; about 51,472 km² of surface.
Coverage
1,179,650 flow cells. Longitude is connected across the map seam; both poles are part of the same graph.
Map resolution
About 262 m between flow cells at the equator. Zooming changes the view of the existing network.
Water
Annual precipitation is partitioned between evaporation and runoff. Lakes contract when inflow cannot sustain a spill. Active flow ends at an ocean or closed basin; possible overflow connections remain available in the global graph.
Climate
Temperature follows latitude and elevation. An approximate circulation transports moisture around complete latitude rings, including windward rainfall and leeward drying. These annual fields change runoff and lake water balances; they are procedural estimates, not a weather forecast.
Snow & glaciers
A monthly temperature-index estimate partitions snowfall, snowmelt and sublimation after two snow spin-up years. The following annual surplus forms ice. Ice exports only along adjacent descending bed elevations; melt returns to the same liquid-water network. Annual ice gain, sea calving and seasonal storage changes are accounted for explicitly. This does not simulate glacier speed or ice-surface deformation.
Volcanic geography
Seeded volcanic arcs and hotspot chains place composite cones, shields, calderas and cinder cones across the sphere. Broad landforms and cooled downhill lava deposits modify the elevation scaffold before oceans, drainage, climate and glaciers are solved. Rust/orange areas are solid geological deposits. This model does not simulate plate motion, eruptions or molten lava.
Land
Only broad drainage elevations are calculated to establish flow direction. There are no detailed terrain meshes, vegetation, buildings or 3D river surfaces.

Lake and pond names refer to their mapped basin inventory (0.35 km² threshold). The blue surface follows estimated annual water availability; exposed basin margins are brown. Dashed waterways indicate seasonal or ephemeral flow estimates. Potential spills are dormant overflow links. Snow values are liquid-water equivalent (SWE), not snow depth. Snow cover and frozen-water shading are annual estimates. Glacier thickness, deformation, evolving shorelines, time-varying river flow and groundwater remain future steps.
